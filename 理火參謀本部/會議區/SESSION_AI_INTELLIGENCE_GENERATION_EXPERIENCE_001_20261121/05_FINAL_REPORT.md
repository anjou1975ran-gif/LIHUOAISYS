## Executive Brief

This meeting finds that **AI systems can plausibly form new, reusable capabilities through interaction, experience abstraction, method recombination and system-level adaptation.** There is already scoped research evidence for test-time learning, procedural memory and skill transfer. However, these results do not establish general autonomous self-evolution. The most important distinction is between remembering or repairing a known task and learning a capability that transfers to unseen tasks.

The meeting's strongest candidate is a qualification-gated experience lifecycle. It does not treat stored transcripts as experience or a generated skill as a validated method. Each promotion requires provenance, outcome evidence, attribution, transfer tests, counterexamples and a rollback path.

The most promising new research direction is a Cross-Method Tension Compiler: selected Method Packs provide distinct observation contracts; a compiler identifies unowned relations or contradictions, forms bridge hypotheses and tests whether a genuinely new method emerges. This remains a hypothesis, not a new formal theory.

B's alternative architectures survived E's cost challenge. E did not veto them. The confrontation revealed that evolution costs are likely relocated rather than eliminated: from some training compute to coordination, data qualification, retrieval, testing, human validation, maintenance, security and externalities.

## 1. Can AI form new capabilities through experience and structural collision?

**Answer: YES, in an operational and conditional sense; NOT YET PROVEN as broad autonomous evolution.**

A system may improve through:
- test-time adaptation within a task;
- external episodic memory and retrieval;
- reflection and procedural skill abstraction;
- cross-trajectory experience abstraction;
- post-training or reinforcement learning updates;
- tool, policy, runtime or architecture changes;
- exchange of verified skills across agents/models.

These are distinct mechanisms. A stored transcript is not learning; a corrected answer is not necessarily transfer; a skill library is not a new model; a weight update is not automatically a better intelligence.

External evidence:
- EMNLP 2025 reports measurable test-time learning in its evaluation, but cumulative gains are less stable than human learning. https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1304/
- ACL Findings 2026 proposes Storage / Reflection / Experience as a useful agent-memory evolution framework. This is a survey framework, not proof that every agent can evolve autonomously. https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2069/
- AFTER (2026) evaluates procedural memory across tasks, roles and model backbones, finding improvements alongside cross-role specialization limits. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23127
- Failure-only evolution research (2026) reports local repair but weak generalization to unseen tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22984

## 2. Can Method Packs become an AI cognitive floor?

**Answer: They can be a reusable observation floor or search prior, but are not intelligence by themselves.**

A Method Pack is distinct from:
- **Knowledge:** what facts or content the system has.
- **Prompt:** temporary instructions and framing for a task.
- **Skill:** a procedure for performing a recurring task.
- **Agent distillation:** a compressed behavior/trajectory/policy extracted from one or more agent executions.
- **Method:** a reusable observation contract that changes the first question, analysis unit, evidence preference, causal framing, blind spots, triggers and suppression conditions.

Method Packs may reduce the need to rediscover how to observe a domain. They can also create productive friction when two disciplines ask different first questions. But their value must be tested by method-shift and held-out transfer tests; they cannot confer truth or specialist authority.

The selected Atlas set was limited to five candidates. Education was used at INTEGRATED_DRAFT standing; AI/ML systems is a Functional Module with an internal PROPOSED label despite the registry's REVIEW_READY standing. Neither discrepancy was silently promoted.

## 3. Can AI create new Methods?

**Answer: A candidate can be generated; admission requires a distinct first-observation shift and evidence of cross-case value.**

A candidate must demonstrate:
1. a genuinely new first observation or analytical unit;
2. a material relation not already subsumed by existing Methods;
3. explicit trigger and suppression conditions;
4. evidence preference and causal model;
5. blind spots, failure modes and negative controls;
6. transfer beyond the cases used to create it;
7. a reviewable version, provenance and retirement path.

**New Method candidate — Experience Conversion & Transfer Audit (ECTA):**
- First observation: Is this improvement recall of a similar case, local repair, a reusable procedure, or a method that transfers across contexts? What evidence and versioned tests support that distinction?
- Source Methods: V0.2.H, V0.2.L, V0.2.M, V0.2.Q, V0.3.M.ai_ml_systems.
- Trigger: claims of learning, skill distillation, persistent memory updates, method formation or cross-agent transfer.
- Suppress: one-off retrieval or a static fact lookup without a learning/transfer claim.
- Evidence: source/version, denominator and task distribution, held-out tasks, counterexamples, regression/forgetting checks and endpoint feedback.
- Blind spots: benchmark leakage, environment drift, hidden confounding, long-tail cases, privacy/IP and model drift.
- Failure mode: local repair or similar-case retrieval is mislabeled as transferable learning.
- Standing: HYPOTHESIS / OPEN / NOT ADMITTED TO METHOD ATLAS.

Distinctness from existing AI/ML, statistics and system-engineering methods still requires review. This is not a formal Atlas addition.

## 4. What architecture is needed for experience exchange?

The Council proposes an **Experience Capsule** rather than raw conversation exchange. Minimum contents:
- capsule identity, source agent/model and version;
- task/environment and scope;
- original episode/trajectory reference;
- action and outcome;
- verifier/feedback and attribution;
- selected Method/skill references;
- transfer conditions and known failure boundaries;
- confidence/standing and unresolved premises;
- cost profile and cost bearer;
- privacy/IP/use permission;
- expiry, revalidation, compatibility and rollback.

Sender provenance does not qualify the receiver's adoption. The receiver must test compatibility and transfer in its own environment. Expert or Agent returns remain evidence, not Council stance.

## 5. How should self-evolution be layered?

The meeting proposes a multi-substrate map, not a single ladder:

1. **Pretraining prior:** broad patterns embedded in model weights.
2. **Post-training / reward optimization:** parameter updates that shape policy or preferences.
3. **In-context adaptation:** temporary use of current context and feedback.
4. **Episodic memory:** stored task-specific events and outcomes.
5. **Procedural memory:** reusable task skills and workflows.
6. **Method abstraction:** cross-trajectory, cross-domain observation rules.
7. **Runtime / architecture evolution:** tools, planners, memory managers, verifiers, orchestration and environment interfaces.
8. **Population / collective evolution:** exchange and selection across models/agents.
9. **Governance and evaluation:** provenance, held-out testing, regression, cost, security, human responsibility and rollback across all layers.

These layers can coexist and interact; none automatically implies the next. Weight updates are durable but costly and vulnerable to forgetting. External memory is cheaper to change but vulnerable to retrieval errors, staleness and poisoned abstractions. A method library may generalize, but only if it survives transfer tests.

ICLR 2025's SCoRe improves self-correction by training with multi-turn online RL and self-generated data; this is evidence for a training pathway, not proof that an ordinary inference-time self-critique loop durably learns. https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/871ac99fdc5282d0301934d23945ebaa-Abstract-Conference.html

## 6. How does structural cost participate in evolution?

**Cost is a design dimension, not a veto.**

Lifecycle cost should include:
- training / fine-tuning / RL compute;
- interaction and environment execution;
- inference and tool orchestration;
- memory storage, retrieval and context use;
- data qualification and curation;
- evaluation, verification and regression testing;
- human/expert review;
- security, privacy/IP and governance;
- maintenance, version migration and retirement;
- failure, recovery and externalities.

A useful proposed comparison unit is:

**Total lifecycle cost per validated transferable capability increment**, not cost per token or number of stored episodes.

The actual break-even point is UNKNOWN: no budget, measured compute, human hours or deployment profile was supplied. The meeting does not estimate an electricity percentage.

## 7. What did the B × E confrontation discover?

B proposed:
- **Capability Ecology:** a population of models, tools and methods that exchange, recombine and are selected through environment-based tests.
- **External Cognitive Substrate / Method Compiler:** a durable experience graph and compiler that composes task-specific cognitive configurations while allowing the model backbone to be replaced.

E's challenge did not reject either architecture. It showed that:
- Capability Ecology relocates cost to coordination, communication, integration, security and selection.
- Method Compiler relocates cost to abstraction quality, provenance, versioning, transfer tests, human validation and maintenance.

**New finding:** Evolution cost is not eliminated; it migrates across layers and cost bearers. The system must reveal who pays and who receives the benefit. B's freedom to explore remains; E's responsibility to account remains.

## 8. Which new theories or experiments should be retained?

Four research candidates survive:

### CANDIDATE-1｜Qualification-Gated Experience Ladder
Raw episode → outcome/feedback → verified diagnosis → procedure/skill → cross-trajectory abstraction → Method candidate → validated Method → optional weight/architecture update.

This extends, rather than replaces, the Storage/Reflection/Experience taxonomy. Every promotion has a qualification gate; no automatic promotion occurs.

### CANDIDATE-2｜Cross-Method Tension Compiler
Select 2–5 Methods by problem routing; collect each first observation, evidence model, blind spots and suppression conditions; construct a typed relation/tension graph; generate a bridge hypothesis; test it against held-out cases and negative controls; output a new Method candidate only if it adds distinct transferable value.

### CANDIDATE-3｜Experience Capsule Exchange
A source-bound, versioned and transfer-qualified exchange format. Receiver-side validation is mandatory.

### CANDIDATE-4｜Experience Conversion & Transfer Audit
A candidate Method to prevent retrieval, repair and procedure reuse from being mislabeled as general learning.

**All four remain HYPOTHESIS / OPEN.** No new formal theory or Method Atlas item is established.

## 9. What remains unanswered?

- Can an LLM derive a robust new method without external human or verifier feedback?
- How far can a method transfer across unrelated domains, model families and environments?
- What evidence distinguishes genuine abstraction from retrieval, prompt sensitivity or benchmark leakage?
- How should contradictory methods be reconciled without forcing premature convergence?
- How should obsolete or harmful memories be retired without erasing useful history?
- Can cross-agent exchange remain secure, auditable and compatible?
- What is the long-term cost and who bears it?
- Can system-level evolution produce capabilities not represented in any single model, and how can that be tested?
- Does parameter-level continual learning outperform external memory/skill/method approaches after total cost and forgetting are included?
