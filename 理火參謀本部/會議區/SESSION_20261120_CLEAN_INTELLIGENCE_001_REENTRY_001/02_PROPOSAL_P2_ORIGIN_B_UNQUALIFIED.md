## 2｜Three substantive product/business options — no winner

### VC-FIELD｜品牌／連鎖門店執行證據
- **Buying role hypothesis:** brand channel operations, franchise compliance/audit manager.
- **Customer problem:** paid promotion executed? shelf labels correct? Can audit and prove disputed execution?
- **Revenue:** legally authorized per-site visit contract, monthly service packages or verified exceptions; no retail data resale required.
- **Source/permission:** signed site visit scope, agreed merchandising checklist, permitted field auditors, photo where permitted, sampling and correction; masking of employees/customers when needed.
- **Cost:** auditors, travel/routing density, spot-check QA, software, client acquisition; extra physical density may improve margins, but unproven.
- **Objection:** retail chains may have capable in-house field teams and digital task tools; site access and incremental WTP unknown.

### VC-BUYER｜電子供應鏈「報價—承諾—收貨」私有核對
- **Buying role hypothesis:** procurement, vendor management and finance/QA in Shenzhen electronics-related firms.
- **Customer problem:** wrong supplier spec/price, late shipment, inconsistent promise/PO/inspection evidence.
- **Revenue:** enterprise setup + private evidence service subscription or charged reconciliation projects; does NOT resell suppliers' confidential prices.
- **Source/permission:** only buyer lawfully authorized supplier quote/PO/logistics/receipt, with vendor contract and third-party processing permits checked.
- **Cost:** customer onboarding, integration, confidential data security, manual exception checks, legal evaluation, sales cycle.
- **Objection:** contracts may restrict external processor; evidence may not create enough incremental ROI; pre-existing ERPs already track some of it.

### VC-P1｜原 P1：合法授權的價格／缺貨監測
- **Buying role hypothesis:** brand distributor/retail merchandising with verified need for extra-market price or stock intelligence.
- **Revenue:** per coverage/refresh interval or customer monthly plan; future licensed feed only with separate grant.
- **Source/permission:** customer-owned POS/ERP or documented merchant/authorized API access and permitted use; no unconsented platform extraction.
- **Cost:** source access licensing, SKU matching, refreshing, QC, updates, legal coverage, customer sales and churn.
- **Objection:** existing retail platforms provide extensive analytics; public price data may lack lawful reuse/unique value.
- **P1 useful part retained:** Evidence Passport, Rights Gate/Quality Gate, non-synthetic observation; old HKD1.8M/90d numerical cap is not a current planning constraint.

## 4｜Cost and recovery relation, without prefixed capital
For each product option *i*, measure:
- Net price received P_i and variable cost VC_i (source licensing, field work, support, verification).
- Contribution CM_i = P_i - VC_i; no break-even possible if CM_i <= 0 under steady assumptions.
- Fixed monthly operating cost F_i → illustrative break-even volume ceil(F_i/CM_i).
- Customer acquisition cash CAC_i → illustrative CAC payback = CAC_i/monthly contribution per customer.
- Approved incremental cash investment I_i → simple operating cash recovery = I_i / positive monthly net cash generated (must account real receipts/payment lags and all necessary expenses before relying on it).
- Field option metrics per *visit*; subscription metrics per *client-month*; procurement projects per *contract milestone*. Do not compare different units without normalization.
- If there is a capital-intensive deployment that demonstrably returns more after full lifecycle costs, it is not excluded by 'too expensive'. If not recoverable, even a tiny SaaS build is a bad investment.

**All prices/costs are unknown. No capital ceiling/floor, no budget authorization.**
